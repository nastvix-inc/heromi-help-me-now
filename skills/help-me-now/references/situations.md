# Situations and age bands

Pick the closest situation below and let its **focus** shape the answer. If nothing fits, use the fallback for what the child is doing. Situations marked with a minimum age apply only from that age.

## Age bands

| Band | Ages | How to adapt |
| - | - | - |
| Toddler | 2-3 | Co-regulation first: your calm body, closeness, physical comfort. Very few words, 2 to 4 per sentence. Name one feeling at most. No reasoning in the moment. |
| Preschool | 4-5 | Name the feeling in simple words. Short explanations. Offer two small choices. Hold the limit kindly. |
| School age | 6-8 | Name the feeling, then help them say it. Offer choices. Short talk about what happened once calm, and solve the next time together. |
| Preteen | 9-12 | Respect their dignity and privacy, especially around others. Fewer instructions, more listening. Ask what would help. Problem-solve together later, not in the heat of it. |

For a child under 2, keep to comfort, closeness and a calm, quiet space. For a teenager, keep the tone respectful and collaborative and do not use the HeroMi link (it is built for ages 2 to 12).

## Child situations

### Transitions (leaving, stopping)

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Leaving the playground | Meltdown when it is time to go | Help them through the change: warning, a goodbye ritual, stay close while they are sad about it. Leaving still happens. |
| Leaving something they love | Meltdown at the end of a visit, party, game | Same as above; acknowledge how fun it was and that endings are hard. |
| Stopping a screen | Meltdown when the show, game or tablet ends | Change support plus frustration: calm "the screen is done", stay close, offer what comes next. |
| General transition (fallback) | Upset at having to switch to something else | Predictability and flexibility: warnings, one step at a time. |

### Frustration (a "no", a blocked goal)

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Did not get what they wanted | Crying, stomping, flopping after a "no" | Let the feeling be big and the limit stay steady. Empathy, not negotiation. |
| "No" in a store | Meltdown at the checkout or in the aisle | Same, plus often tired or hungry. Move somewhere quieter; ignore onlookers. |
| Toy taken away by another child | Crying, grabbing back | Frustration and impulse control: keep hands safe, name it, help with a turn. |
| General frustration (fallback) | Frustrated, crying | Name the frustration, stay steady, help them try again or let it go. |

### Overwhelm and exhaustion

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Overwhelmed in public | Meltdown in a noisy, crowded place | Less input: move away from noise and light, lower your voice, fewer words. |
| Meltdown after daycare | Falls apart on pick-up or at home afterwards | Rest and closeness: a snack, quiet, you. Falling apart with you means they feel safe with you. |
| General overwhelm (fallback) | Upset and cannot calm down | Bring the intensity down: slow breathing with them, quiet, closeness. |

### Refusing (routines and cooperation)

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Bedtime | Stalling, running away, "I'm not tired" | Cooperation through connection and routine; small choices within the routine. |
| Getting dressed | Refuses clothes, runs off | Autonomy: two choices, make it a game or race, help without a battle. |
| Brushing teeth | Clamps mouth, runs off | Autonomy and playfulness: let them do a part, a song, a turn to brush your teeth. |
| General refusal (fallback) | "No!" to a request | Connection before direction, one clear request, a small choice. |

### Aggression

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Hitting or kicking the parent | Hits, kicks, bites, throws | Keep everyone safe first: block calmly, move away, "I won't let you hit." Then calm, then talk later. |
| Hitting a sibling | Fights, hurting a brother or sister | Safety for both, then repair once calm. No blaming one child in the moment. |

### Shutting down

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Frozen or shut down | Goes quiet, hides, will not answer or look | Gentle reconnection and safety: stay near, no pressure to talk, soft voice, time. |

### Fear and worry

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Scared or anxious (fallback) | Clingy, crying, afraid | Safety and reassurance: "You're safe. I'm here." Do not dismiss the fear or force it. |

### Other children

| Situation | Min age | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - | - |
| Sharing breakdown | | Tears over a toy or a turn with another child | Frustration and social repair: turns, a timer, help both children. |
| Conflict with one child | | Hitting or shouting at another child | Keep hands safe, then help repair once calm. |
| Hurt by another child | | Scared after being hit or pushed | Safety and reassurance, then restore confidence: it was not their fault. |
| Hurt by another child and shut down | | Frozen or silent after being hurt | Gentle reconnection, then reassurance. |
| Left out | 4+ | Crying because others will not play with them | Belonging: being left out hurts; your love is steady; talk about it later. |
| Left out and shut down | 5+ | Withdrawn after being excluded | Reconnect gently first, then belonging. |
| Teased or treated meanly | 5+ | Upset after unkind words | Repair their confidence; it says nothing about who they are. |
| "It's not fair" | 4+ | Upset about unfair rules or turns | Take the fairness feeling seriously, then help them process it. |

### School (5+)

| Situation | Typical signs | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Refusing to go to school | Will not get ready, will not go in | Safety and reassurance first, then autonomy within the plan. Stay calm and warm; ask later what feels hard. |
| After-school collapse | Falls apart at home after holding it together all day | Rest and closeness before questions and homework. |
| Upset about a grade or mistake | "I'm stupid", tears over schoolwork | Repair shame: effort and learning matter more than the result; mistakes are how brains grow. |
| Hard goodbye at school | Clinging and crying at drop-off | Separation reassurance: a short, confident goodbye ritual and a clear "I'll be back at...". |

## Parent situations

| The parent feels | Because | Focus |
| - | - | - |
| Overwhelmed, touched out | Too many demands at once | Shrink the moment: one thing at a time, drop what can wait. |
| | Too much noise or touch | Reduce input: step to a quieter spot, headphones, cold water. |
| | No break at all | Name the depletion; find a micro-break in the next hour. |
| | Parenting alone right now | Acknowledge the load; lower the bar for today. |
| Angry, about to lose it | The child will not listen | Pause before speaking; lower the voice; one short request. |
| | Repeated defiance | It is not personal; step back, breathe, hold the limit calmly later. |
| | Patience is gone | Put space in first: step away for a minute once the child is safe. |
| Guilty or ashamed | Yelled at the child | Repair is what matters; give a simple repair script. |
| | Lost patience | One moment does not define them; plan a small reconnection. |
| | Felt judged by others | Other people's looks are not the measure; the child needs them, not the audience. |
| Exhausted, depleted | Not enough sleep | Lower the bar; survival mode is allowed today. |
| | No break | Find the smallest possible rest; ask for help if possible. |
| | Parenting alone | Acknowledge the weight; one thing that makes today easier. |
| Not sure what they feel | | General grounding and self-compassion. |
